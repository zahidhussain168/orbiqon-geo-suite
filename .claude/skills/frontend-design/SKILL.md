## Overview

This document serves as a comprehensive guide for creating distinctive visual design work. It emphasizes moving beyond templated defaults to deliver work with a clear point of view tailored to each specific project.

## Key Principles

**Ground Design in Subject Matter**: Before designing, establish the concrete subject, its audience, and the page's primary purpose. Draw inspiration from the subject's own materials, instruments, and vernacular.

**Hero Element**: Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world, in whatever form makes sense for it. This could be a headline, image, animation, or interactive moment, not necessarily a templated metric display.

**Typography as Personality**: Select display and body typefaces deliberately for each project. Establish a clear type scale with intentional weights, widths, and spacing that becomes memorable rather than neutral.

**Structural Honesty**: Use structural devices like numbering or dividers only when they encode genuine information about content, such as actual sequences or timelines, not as pure decoration.

**Thoughtful Motion**: Deploy animation strategically to serve the subject matter, considering page-load sequences, scroll triggers, and hover states. Avoid scattered effects that suggest artificial generation.

## Design Process

Work in two passes: first develop a compact design plan with color palette, typography, layout concept, and signature element. Then critique this plan against the brief to ensure it's truly specific to the project rather than a default approach.

## Avoiding Common Defaults

The document identifies three prevalent AI-generated aesthetic clusters to sidestep: cream backgrounds with serif display fonts, near-black backgrounds with bright accent colors, and broadsheet-style dense layouts. These become defaults rather than considered choices.

## Written Content

Copy functions as design material. Use active voice, specific language, and conversational tone. Label controls clearly, explain errors helpfully, and treat empty states as invitations rather than failures.
